## This checkpoint

- **Safari blank readers (root cause of the "TV opens the old page" report).** `TvScreenReader` and `MonitorScreenReader` used drei's `Html transform` mode. Its inner layer is scaled to metres (about 0.006) and magnified back through CSS perspective; WebKit rasterises the layer at the tiny scale, so Safari showed a blank TV and monitor while the reader's controls stayed tappable. Taps on the blank TV landed on invisible controls, most often the `Teaching & training` link below the screen, whose Education panel is the legacy `/education` content. Both readers now use a screen-aligned overlay (`center` + viewport-aware `distanceFactor`); they only mount once the camera faces the screen head-on, so geometry matches the bezel to 0.1 px in Chromium and paints in WebKit. Chromium-only QA had never exercised this path.
- **Header = office title.** The upper-left brand block (mark, name, caption) is gone. The italic office title with the `TAKMD / A PLACE TO THINK` kicker is the page `h1` upper-left; guided views rename it and keep the close control beside it. Guided-view buttons stay lower-right; Living CV / Contact stay upper-right.

## Phone memory (2026-09-17, after the re-added home-screen app still restarted)

Picking another lecture in the archive sent the re-added home-screen app back to the opening screen. No reload, navigation or error reproduces in WebKit emulation; the room simply exceeds a phone's memory. Measured on the iPhone 14 viewport: 153 textures worth about 858 MB on the GPU (fifteen at 2048², one book spine at 2100×2800 = 30 MB), 50 MB of geometry, 7 MB of shadow maps, 191 MB of JS heap. iOS kills a web content process near 1.5 GB and a standalone app relaunches at its start URL, which is the "error then reset" the owner sees. Chrome shows the same pressure as stutter.

`StudioScene` now caps `gl.capabilities.maxTextureSize` at 1024 on phones (2048 on desktop). Three resizes any larger image on a canvas before upload, so the cap reaches every loader; 59 textures are resized at upload on a phone, the 2048² ones to 1024². The HTML readers are unaffected. Measure GPU memory with the estimate script in `.omo/evidence/phone-texture-cap-2026-09-17/` (source dimensions, so it does not see the cap; count the `Texture has been resized` console warnings instead). The next steps of the weight plan — fewer lights and shadow casters on phones, deferred loading of off-screen models, meshopt/KTX2 — are not started.

## Phone render budget: released and reverted the same morning (2026-09-17)

The frame cap (30/20 fps), the rect-area light cut (20 → 8) and the half-resolution exterior were measured on a Mac GPU and shipped; on the phone they read as slower and flatter, and the whisky cabinet lost its night light. The owner's verdict: no perceptible gain, real loss of the room's character. Reverted in full. Lesson recorded: Mac frame times say nothing about a phone; a frame cap makes nothing faster; do not remove lights or resolution for speed. The texture cap (memory, crash fix) stays. The phone bottleneck to attack next is per-frame CPU work — draw calls (792 per pass, three passes) and geometry — by merging static meshes and by simplifying or deferring models, with quality held constant and every change judged on the phone.
