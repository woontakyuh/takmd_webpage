## Static mesh merge (2026-09-17)

`StaticMerge` sweeps the scene once a second and draws groups of at least five static opaque `MeshStandardMaterial` meshes that share material properties, shadow flags and render order as one mesh, anchored to the scene or to the nearest `Furniture layout` group so rearranging still moves them. Meshes with pointer handlers anywhere above them are never touched, so hover, click and the arrangement mode are unaffected; the originals stay in the tree hidden and React keeps owning them. A group is released and rebuilt if any original moves, changes material or leaves the scene, so light-mode and palette changes simply re-merge within a second.

Result: 75 meshes → 9 (walls, shelf boards, rails, wall boxes, window frames). Draw calls per pass 792 → 760 on the phone overview, 888 → 823 on desktop; pixel difference across four views 0.03 %. The honest gain is 4–7 %; the interactive objects hold the rest of the calls and are out of scope for an automatic merge. The tinted diagnostic (`.omo/evidence/static-merge-2026-09-17/merge-*-tinted.png`) shows exactly which pieces merge.

`scene.userData.staticMerge` reports `{ sweeps, groups, released, members }` for QA. Verified: moving a merged original releases its group on the next sweep and re-merges it at the new place; a light-mode change leaves the nine groups standing. Beware when testing: the app hides some meshes itself (a wall facing the camera, the TV screen behind its reader), so pick members from `members`, not from any hidden mesh.
